How the ball flies

The ball feels gravity, drag along its motion through the air, and Magnus lift at right angles to that motion and to its spin axis. How big those forces are depends on the spin ratio S = ωr/V and the Reynolds number.

The model and where it comes from

  • Drag. Dimples bring the "drag crisis" down to Re ≈ 40,000–60,000. Above that, drag stays nearly constant (Choi, Jeon & Choi 2006; Bearman & Harvey 1976) and rises with spin.
  • Lift. CL = a·Sb, the form Smits & Smith (1994) found in the wind tunnel.
  • Spin decay. The spin slows under an aerodynamic torque with CM ≈ 0.012·S (Tavares, Shannon & Melvin 1998, from radar).
  • Fitting. The constants were fitted to TrackMan's tour-average trajectories, and to its measured effects of wind, temperature and altitude. The fit came out at CL = 0.49·S0.48 (Smits & Smith: 0.54·S0.4) and a torque coefficient of 0.0121, which independently matches Tavares's 0.012.

Measured effects of wind and air (TrackMan studies)

  • 20 mph on a 300-yard drive: into the wind −41 yd (model −50); downwind +33 (model +30).
  • 20 mph on a 6-iron: into the wind −39 (model −43); downwind +23 (model +16). A headwind hurts more than a tailwind helps.
  • Temperature: about +2.5 yd per 10°F (model +1.5).
  • Altitude: about 2 yd per 1,000 ft (model +2.3).

Wind

  • Height. Wind is slower near the ground. It grows with height by the log law u(z) ∝ ln(z/z₀), where z₀ is the roughness of the ground: about 1 cm on links, 3 cm on parkland, 30 cm among trees. Weather stations measure at 10 m. The yellow flag shows the wind at 2 m, which is what you feel on the tee and is much less. A ball peaking at 30 m flies in more wind than either.
  • Gusts. These are spectral turbulence (Kolmogorov spectrum) carried downwind at the mean speed (Taylor's hypothesis). Turbulence intensity is about 1/ln(z/z₀) (Eurocode EN 1991-1-4). "40 shots in gusts" hits the same shot into different gusts so you can see the spread.

Air and rain

  • Air density. It comes from temperature, pressure (the standard atmosphere for altitude) and humidity. Moist air is lighter than dry air.
  • Rain. The ball sweeps up the raindrops in its path, from Marshall–Palmer liquid water content, and takes on their momentum. A wet ball also carries about 0.1 g of water. Even heavy rain costs a drive only a couple of yards in the air.
  • Where rain matters more. Its big effects are at impact, where water between face and ball gives lower spin and "flyers" (the Launch Lab), and on landing, where soft wet ground stops the ball (the Roll Lab). MyGolfSpy's wet-ball tests found carry changed by less than a yard while spin dropped 11–30%.
