How a divot is worked out

  • The swing. The leading edge swings on an arc about 1.4–1.6 m in radius, which matches TrackMan's rule of thumb that the attack angle changes about 1° per inch of ball position. Where the arc bottoms out (ahead of or behind the ball) and how deep it goes set the attack angle and how much turf it meets. That's why long divots are also deep ones. The ground can slow the club and push it upwards: the hands and shaft act as a spring, and the hands keep driving.
  • Cutting. The face cuts soil like a tillage blade: Reece's earthmoving equation with the McKyes & Ali wedge factors, covering soil weight, cohesion, stickiness to steel, and the soil being thrown at club speed. At golf speeds, throwing the soil is most of the force.
  • Sole and leading edge. These press on the floor of the cut. That pressure comes from Bekker sinkage (Caldwell & McPhee 2024 use the same model for a clubhead), plus the soil's bearing capacity under a blunt edge. That's what bounces an iron off hard, dry ground and lets a sand wedge's bounce skim.
  • Wetness. Wetness changes everything through the soil's water: the suction stress of Lu, Godt & Wu (2010) on van Genuchten curves. Dry clay is hard and wet clay is soft and sticky, while sand hardly changes. Turf roots (Wu–Waldron root cohesion in the top few cm) hold a divot together, but lose grip in waterlogged soil.
  • The ball. If the club meets turf first, some of it ends up between face and ball: less speed and much less spin (fat). If the leading edge is above the ball's equator it's thin. Launch comes from simple TrackMan-shaped relationships; the Launch Lab will replace these.
  • Divot types. Divots come out as an intact strip, partly broken, shattered or mud (after the divot types of Patton et al. 2009). The divot is then thrown forward.

Logged as damage

Every divot becomes a 24-byte record: where it is, which way it points, length, width, depth, kind, who made it, when, and whether it was put back or filled with sand and seed. The crater you see is rebuilt from that record, as every player's device would rebuild it. Each player's habits are three bytes per club: depth, low point and how much it wanders.

Simplified

The cut is 2D in the swing direction, with the width added afterwards. There's no heel or toe digging and no twist of the face. Grass in front of the ball (flyers) belongs to the Launch Lab.
