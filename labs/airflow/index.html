How the wind is worked out

The wind over the hole is solved in 3D on a grid of 5 m × 2 m (height) × 5 m cells, 400 m long, 130 m wide and 40 m high. It runs continuously, so you see it settle and then keep gusting.
  • The flow. Incompressible air: what flows into a cell flows out. The numerics are "stable fluids" (Stam 1999) on a staggered grid (Harlow & Welch 1965).
  • Trees. Foliage slows the air with the standard vegetation drag, F = −Cd·a·|U|·U, where a is the leaf area density (Shaw & Schumann 1992; Finnigan 2000). Summer crowns are dense, autumn crowns lighter, and in winter only the twigs and branches are left.
  • Mixing. Turbulence carries momentum down from the faster air above, through an eddy viscosity from a mixing length (Prandtl, Blackadar). The strong shear at the top of the trees is what refills the sheltered zone behind them, the "mixing layer" of Raupach, Finnigan & Brunet (1996). The ground drags on the lowest air through the log law.
  • Incoming wind. It arrives with the open-ground log profile, slower near the ground and faster higher up, plus gusts.
  • Swirls. These are helped by vorticity confinement (Fedkiw, Stam & Jensen 2001), because the coarse grid smears small eddies out.

Checked against field measurements

For a dense tree belt 10 m tall, the wind at 2.5 m in its lee drops to 37% of the open-ground value just behind it, 54% at 5 heights, 71% at 10 and 83% at 20, and is still recovering at 30. That matches the field picture: a reduced zone from about 5 heights upwind to 30–35 heights downwind, and still about 80% at 20 heights (Heisler & DeWalle 1988; Cleugh 1998). The model's minimum comes slightly earlier than measured, at 2 heights against 3–5.

What it does to the ball

The ball flies through the computed wind using the Ball Flight Lab's model. The same shot on an open course in the same wind is shown dashed for comparison. Wind measured on the tee at head height can be a fraction of what the ball meets at 25–30 m, and the landing zone can be sheltered or funnelled. The profile chart shows those layers.

For the game

The average field can be stored as a few horizontal layers, say at 2, 5, 10, 20 and 35 m, on a 5–10 m grid, for each wind direction. That's a small table per hole that the game looks up while the ball flies.

Simplified

The cells are 5 m, so single trees are only a few cells across: crowns are blocks of drag, not branches. Small eddies are smeared out. The atmosphere is taken as neutral (no sun-warmed or night-time stable air). The ground is flat.
