Grass species

  • Browntop bent and red fescue are the fine grasses of UK greens; annual meadow-grass (Poa annua) dominates many of them. Bent greens carry roughly 70,000–130,000 shoots per m² in winter and 150,000–220,000 in summer (trial of 21 browntop bent cultivars). Fewer, larger shoots grow as grass is cut higher, and the densities here are approximate.
  • Perennial ryegrass has broad, glossy leaves (2–5 mm wide, NC State). It stripes strongly and is common on parkland fairways and roughs. Fine fescues have bristle-like rolled leaves: wiry, upright and wispy in links rough.

The physics

  • Wind near the ground. Above the turf, wind follows the log law. Inside the canopy it dies away exponentially (Inoue 1963), faster in dense, stiff swards (Cionco). The grass tips only see about 10–16% of the wind measured at 10 m.
  • Bending. Each blade is a cantilever under drag. How much it bends depends on the Cauchy number, ρ Cd b u² L³ / 2EI. As a blade leans it turns edge-on and catches less wind (reconfiguration: Gosselin, de Langre & Machado-Almeida 2010; de Langre et al. 2012). Bending goes with length cubed, so greens, fairways and the first cut stay still in any wind, while long rough rolls.
  • Flopping. A thin blade standing up buckles under its own weight beyond a critical length, where μgL³/EI = 7.84 (Greenhill's self-buckling column). That length is about 13–16 cm for flat-leaved grasses and about 30 cm for wiry rolled fescue. Long ryegrass rough lies over; fescue rough stands up.
  • Waves. Each patch sways at its blades' natural frequency, 0.56/L² √(EI/μ). That's hundreds of Hz for mown turf, a few Hz for 10 cm rough, and about 1 Hz for long rough and seed-head stems. Gusts sweeping across make travelling waves, called honami. Py, de Langre & Moulia 2006 found such waves on crops lock to the plants' own frequency.
  • Mowing stripes come from blades laid over by the mower's roller. Blades leaning away from you show their waxy face and look light; blades leaning towards you show tips and shade and look dark (Golf Course Industry). Walk round to the other end and the stripes swap.

What's drawn

A green has over 100,000 shoots per m², far too many to draw. Blades here are drawn densely near the camera and widened to give about the right cover, with the turf colour underneath. A golf ball in each area shows scale and how far it sits down.
