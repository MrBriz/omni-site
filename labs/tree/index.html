How these trees are grown

Nothing here is a stored model or picture. Each tree is grown from a seed number by a few rules taken from the research below, so every seed gives a different tree of the same species. Drag to orbit, pinch to zoom.

Branches compete for space

  • The crown's volume is filled with invisible target points. Branch tips grow toward the points near them, and a point is removed once a branch reaches it, so branches spread out to fill the crown without crossing. Changing the crown outline and the pull of gravity gives oak, birch, poplar and pine. Runions, Lane & Prusinkiewicz 2007, space colonization
  • Later work adds buds competing for light and branch shedding; a natural next step for this lab. Palubicki et al. 2009, self-organizing tree models

Conifers follow a plan

  • The spruce has one straight leader with rings of branches whose length, angle and sag depend on height, described by a handful of numbers. Weber & Penn 1995, Creation and Rendering of Realistic Trees

Thickness: Leonardo's rule

  • Where a branch splits, the parent is as thick as its children combined. Switch Leaves off, or pick Winter, to see it. Eloy 2011, Leonardo's rule and wind-induced stresses in trees

Leaves

  • Twigs and sprigs spiral round their branch by the golden angle (137.5°), as real shoots do. Leaf outlines are simple formulas per species, drawn once as a sprig; a few thousand sprigs make a crown.
  • The crown is lit as one soft volume and darkened toward the inside, a standard trick from game foliage.
