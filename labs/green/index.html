How the putting is worked out

  • Rolling. A rolling ball slows at (5/7)·g·(ρ + slope). The 5/7 is because part of its energy is in its spin (Penner 2002, "The physics of putting"). The rolling resistance ρ comes from the Stimpmeter: the ramp releases the ball at 1.83 m/s, so a green that rolls S feet has a = v₀²/2S. Stimp 10 is 0.55 m/s².
  • Skid. A putt starts out sliding. Friction spins it up until it is rolling, which takes about the first 15–20% of the distance (Penner 2002).
  • Grain. The ball rolls faster down-grain and slower into it, with a small sideways push from the leaning blades. Grain is strongest on Bermuda and slight on bent.
  • The hole. The ball drops if, while crossing the hole along its chord, it falls further than its own radius before reaching the far edge (Holmes 1991). Straight at the middle that gives a top speed of 1.64 m/s (Holmes: 1.63). Faster and off-centre it lips out or goes over.
  • The read. The start line and speed are found by trial putts (a secant search) so the ball goes through the middle at the chosen pace. Firm finishes about 40 cm past (Pelz's 17 inches); die-it-in just reaches.

What's lying on the green

Unrepaired pitch marks dent the surface, and weeds and thin patches add bumps and drag. Sand splashed from the bunker slows the ball. Leaves, twigs and worm casts knock it off line and take speed off when it runs into them. "Clean my line" lifts the loose bits on your line, brushes sand off and repairs pitch marks, as the Rules allow, and it shows who left the marks. Everything is stored as the same 24-byte records as bunker footprints and divots.

Ways of showing a putt

  • TV trace: the ball's path coloured by speed, with the aim line and the break point, as on golf broadcasts.
  • Projected line: the line with moving pace dots and where it would stop, like the PuttView projector.
  • Slope map: a slope heat map in AimPoint-style colours, contours every 2 cm and fall-line arrows, like a green book.
  • Capture window: every start line and speed that goes in, the "make zone" used in putting research.
  • Spread: 30 putts with a player's usual errors in aim and pace.
Heights are drawn ×3 so the slopes are easy to see.
