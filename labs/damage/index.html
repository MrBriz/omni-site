The course damage log

Every divot, pitch mark, bunker footprint and splash crater is a 24-byte record: what it is, where, which way, how big and deep, a seed for its exact shape, who made it, when, and whether they mended it. The course is generated procedurally, so these records are all the game needs to draw the damage on anyone's device.
  • Mending later is another 24-byte record naming the mark it mends: rake, repair, put back or sand and seed, and who did it.
  • Syncing two devices just pools their records. They can be swapped in any order and everyone ends up with the same course. "Two phones, then sync" checks that.
  • Healing is worked out, not stored. Cool-season grass grows at a rate set by the day's mean temperature: the growth-potential curve used by PACE Turf (Gelernter & Stowell 2005). In an MK summer that's ~85% of its best; in January ~2%.
    • A pitch mark repaired properly recovers fastest (Fry et al. 2005). Left alone, it takes about 18 summer days (Young, Richardson & Karcher 2010), and ball marks are worse when the green is wet.
    • A divot put back roots in about two weeks. Sand and seed takes 4–5 weeks (CDGA divot studies). Left open, it takes weeks to months.
    • Bunker marks never grow back. They stay until raked, and rain slowly smooths them.
    Healed marks can be dropped from the log by any device, and they all agree.
  • Who's to blame: the table counts what each player made, left open, mended for themselves and mended for others, plus the area of damage they've left. Tap a player to show only their marks, then make them mend theirs.

The play

Groups of four tee off every ten minutes. Each player's skill sets their drive and scatter, and their divot habits match the Divot Lab presets. Their tidiness is the chance they mend their own marks, and tidy players also fix someone else's. Wet ground means bigger, muddier divots and deeper pitch marks. The greenkeeper's morning round rakes the bunkers, fills half the open divots and repairs some pitch marks.
