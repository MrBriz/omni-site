How the lie changes the launch

  • The impact. The face meets the ball at the dynamic loft and slides across it. Friction makes the ball roll up the face and spin, as long as there's enough of it; otherwise the ball slides and spins less. This is the standard oblique-impact model of a sphere struck by a heavy face (e.g. Penner 2001; Cross 2005, whose tangential "spin overshoot" adds about 20% when the ball grips). It's tuned to TrackMan's tour averages: a clean 7-iron gives 121 mph, 16.7° and 6,900 rpm.
  • What's between face and ball. A clean grooved face has a friction coefficient of about 0.4. Grass trapped between face and ball brings it towards 0.07, and a water film towards 0.06. Grooves win some back unless they are clogged. Grass also cushions the blow a little. With grass trapped, the ball slides and launches higher with less spin, especially with wedges: a flyer. This matches the USGA's groove and spin research showing large spin losses from the rough.
  • The grass on the way in. The head ploughs through the grass before the ball. The model uses the canopy's density × the volume the head sweeps, accelerated to club speed, which slows the club. Grass catches the hosel more than the toe, which twists the face shut, so rough goes left. Into the grain (grass leaning towards you) means more grass and more twist; down the grain means less.
  • How the ball sits. A ball sitting up has little grass above the contact point. A ball sitting down has a lot.
  • Slopes. Uphill adds launch, and downhill takes it away. On a sidehill lie the loft tilts the face: it points left with the ball above your feet by atan(tan loft × sin slope), and right with it below. The ball then curves, following the D-plane.
  • Mud. A lump of mud on the side of the ball stays on that side as it spins and pushes the ball away from it, as golfers find. Mud on the top or bottom spins round and mostly adds drag. Mud where the face hits also cuts friction and spin.
  • An old divot. The ball sits below the surface, the hands go forward and the club must dig: lower launch, less spin, a little less speed.
